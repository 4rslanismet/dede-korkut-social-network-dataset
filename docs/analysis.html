Null model validation

12 / 36 tests remain significant after Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction (α=0.05). Community modularity is validated as a non-random signal (not a degree-sequence artifact) in 7 of 9 network specifications tested; degree assortativity is not supported in any network (an earlier “disassortative” claim is withdrawn). The kinship network (G3) shows no deviation from its null, but it is a forest, so its clustering and transitivity tests are uninformative.

How to read “7 of 9”: the 9 tested networks are related, partly nested specifications of one corpus (for example G1 and G2 are subsets/variants of G0), so these are not 7 independent replications. Observed modularity is a single Louvain partition (seed 42, unweighted); each of the 1000 randomized graphs (degree-preserving edge swaps) gets one Louvain run.

Sensitivity analysis

Rank-correlation of centrality across 6 network-construction choices (descriptive). Weighted betweenness uses shortest-path distance = 1/tie strength; the “unweighted” arm is hop-count betweenness (DEC-017). 3 choices change rankings noticeably ('noticeable' = at least one measure with Spearman rho < 0.95; otherwise 'negligible'): person plus group vs person only (mean ρ 0.911, min 0.892); weighted vs unweighted (mean ρ 0.912, min 0.849); groups included vs excluded (mean ρ 0.952, min 0.949). The other 3 barely matter (ρ ≥ 0.98). The mean ρ of the noticeable choices spans only 0.041, the comparisons use different node sets, and differences between correlations were not tested, so none of them is singled out as the most consequential.

Structural robustness

Removing nodes at random needs 25.1% of the 311 G0 nodes (78 nodes) before the largest connected component falls below 50% of all 311 G0 nodes; measured against the initial giant component (261 nodes) instead, it needs 30.9%. Targeted removal is far more disruptive: checked after every removal, degree-targeted removal crosses the all-nodes threshold after 6–7 nodes (median 7; range over 200 random tie-breaks among equal-degree nodes) and hop-count-betweenness-targeted removal after 6 nodes. The coarser 6-node checkpoint grid reports 12 vs 6 nodes, but that gap is a resolution/tie-break artifact, so no claim is made that one targeted strategy is more destructive than the other; the supported conclusion is only that targeted removal is substantially more disruptive than random removal (G0_full only). This describes graph connectivity only, not narrative resilience.
